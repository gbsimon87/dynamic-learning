# Coming changes — revised national curriculum from September 2028 (England)

**Source:** [Curriculum and Assessment Review Final Report: government response](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b2a4a14b040dfe82922ea/Government_response_to_the_Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review.pdf) (PDF, November 2025), attached to [the GOV.UK publication page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report-government-response) — Department for Education
**Retrieved:** 2026-11-02
**Status:** policy paper; not statutory. Every other file in this folder is still the law until the revised curriculum replaces it
**Licence:** Open Government Licence v3.0

This is a watch note, not a programme of study. The government has said it
will publish a revised national curriculum by spring 2027 for first teaching
from September 2028. As of 2026-11-02, GOV.UK has no draft programmes of
study. When they appear, save them as new files and keep the current ones
until 2028.

Quotations are verbatim, with line breaks rejoined. They are the passages that
bear on years 5 and 6 or on subjects we build.

---

## Timeline

> we will refresh the programmes of study for each curriculum subject in line with the Review’s recommendations and publish a revised national curriculum in 2027 for first teaching in 2028.

> we have decided that we should fully implement the new full national curriculum for first teaching from September 2028. We will ensure that schools have plenty of time to plan to implement the changes. We therefore will aim to publish the final revised national curriculum by spring 2027 – giving schools four terms to prepare for the changes.

## Languages (our French)

> We will work with experts to strengthen the primary languages curriculum and clarify expectations for pupil progress, as well as define core content in French, German and Spanish.

French is likely to get defined core content. Topics we build before then
should stick to the current statutory bullets in
[key-stage-2-languages.md](key-stage-2-languages.md) and then be checked
against the new content.

## English grammar (Years 5 and 6)

> We will therefore review the grammar content in the primary curriculum, and the Standards and Testing Agency (STA) will review the grammar, punctuation and spelling assessment to address these concerns. We will also consider whether the sequencing of some of the grammatical content should be changed, including whether some of the more complex concepts should be moved into key stage 3.

Two of the concepts the response names are fronted adverbials (Year 4) and
the subjunctive mood (Year 6, in
[year-5-and-6-english.md](year-5-and-6-english.md)).

## Primary assessment (Year 6 tests)

> We recognise the importance of grammar for clarity of communication, comprehension and personal impact, so will continue to test this at the end of primary. We will also ask STA to amend the test to ensure it better reflects assessing composition and application of grammar and punctuation.

> When the curriculum has been revised, STA will revisit the assessments to see what relevant changes need to be made to them.

## Citizenship becomes compulsory in primary

> By making citizenship compulsory in primary, we will introduce all pupils to key content including media literacy, financial literacy, law and rights, democracy and government and climate education to enable children to be informed and active participants in society.

Citizenship is not a primary subject today (it starts at key stage 3), so
there is nothing to save yet.
