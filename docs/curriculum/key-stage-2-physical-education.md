Statutory text is reproduced verbatim. Do not paraphrase it — the wording is
the specification. Physical education is set out per key stage, not per year:
key stage 2 covers years 3 to 6, so years 5 and 6 have no content of their
own. The purpose of study and aims, which apply to every key stage, come
first. *Swimming and water safety* may be taught in key stage 1 or key stage
2, so it is kept here too. The mapping to our dataset is at the end.

The text follows the PDF; how the gov.uk web page differs is listed at the end.

## Purpose of study

A high-quality physical education curriculum inspires all pupils to succeed and excel in competitive sport and other physically-demanding activities. It should provide opportunities for pupils to become physically confident in a way which supports their health and fitness. Opportunities to compete in sport and other activities build character and help to embed values such as fairness and respect.

## Aims

The national curriculum for physical education aims to ensure that all pupils:

- develop competence to excel in a broad range of physical activities
- are physically active for sustained periods of time
- engage in competitive sports and activities
- lead healthy, active lives.

### Attainment targets

By the end of each key stage, pupils are expected to know, apply and understand the matters, skills and processes specified in the relevant programme of study.

Schools are not required by law to teach the example content in [square brackets].

### Subject content

## Key stage 2

Pupils should continue to apply and develop a broader range of skills, learning how to use them in different ways and to link them to make actions and sequences of movement. They should enjoy communicating, collaborating and competing with each other. They should develop an understanding of how to improve in different physical activities and sports and learn how to evaluate and recognise their own success.

Pupils should be taught to:

- use running, jumping, throwing and catching in isolation and in combination
- play competitive games, modified where appropriate [for example, badminton, basketball, cricket, football, hockey, netball, rounders and tennis], and apply basic principles suitable for attacking and defending
- develop flexibility, strength, technique, control and balance [for example, through athletics and gymnastics]
- perform dances using a range of movement patterns
- take part in outdoor and adventurous activity challenges both individually and within a team
- compare their performances with previous ones and demonstrate improvement to achieve their personal best.

## Swimming and water safety

All schools must provide swimming instruction either in key stage 1 or key stage 2.

In particular, pupils should be taught to:

- swim competently, confidently and proficiently over a distance of at least 25 metres
- use a range of strokes effectively [for example, front crawl, backstroke and breaststroke]
- perform safe self-rescue in different water-based situations.

The statutory text names the water safety code for swimming only through the requirement to "perform safe self-rescue in different water-based situations". The four-step Water Safety Code taught in the Swimming and Water Safety topic (Stop and think, Stay together, Float to live, Call 999) comes from the national water safety campaign, not from this programme of study. The topic asks nothing beyond those four steps and "never swim alone", and it never teaches rescuing someone by entering the water.

Left for Year 6: further competitive games and rules (for example more detail on tactics in invasion games and the full rules of cricket), harder time, distance and score comparisons, longer movement sequences and dances linked into performances, orienteering with scale and bearings, and the Year 6 science link to healthy living ([year-6-science.md](year-6-science.md)). Everything above can be revisited in Year 6 with harder questions, since the programme repeats across years 3 to 6.
