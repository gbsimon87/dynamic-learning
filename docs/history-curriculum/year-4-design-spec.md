# Year 4 History: design spec

**Status:** Approved by the owner, 2026-10-12; implemented and verified the same day.
**Tracker:** [HISTORY_TRACKER.md](HISTORY_TRACKER.md). Read it first when resuming.
**Shared spec:** [shared-design-spec.md](shared-design-spec.md): engine, images, tone,
registration and testing for all years. This file holds only what is specific to Year 4.
**Source:** [key-stage-2-history.md](../curriculum/key-stage-2-history.md) (statutory, verbatim).

## 1. Goal

Year 4 History in Curriculum Mode: two units, 18 topics, 72 challenges, for children
aged 8–9.

**Success means:** a Year 4 child can choose History, work through all 18 topics in
order, and earn progress, rewards and certificates as in other subjects; every question
is answerable from its story card and source; every fact traces to a reference; every
image has a recorded open licence.

## 2. Units (approved 2026-10-12)

Year 4 continues the British story from where Year 3 ends (the Romans leaving):

- Britain's settlement by Anglo-Saxons and Scots
- the Viking and Anglo-Saxon struggle for the Kingdom of England to the time of Edward
  the Confessor

The story stops at Edward the Confessor's death in 1066. The Norman Conquest is key
stage 3 and gets only a one-sentence "what happened next" on the last story card.

Ancient Greece, a non-European society, the local history study and the "beyond 1066"
theme are left for Years 5–6, which aren't planned. Record this in the KS2 mapping
section as a deliberate gap.

## 4. Learning design

Uses the shared model: story card, then five rounds (shared spec §3).

### The four challenges

Same shape as Year 3, with one step up in slot 3: Year 4 starts asking **who made a
source and why**, which is the KS2 aim about contrasting interpretations.

| Slot | Working name | Focus | Main rounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meet it | Key facts and words | choice, sort |
| 2 | When was it? | Chronology across 410–1066; Anglo-Saxon and Viking periods | order, timeline |
| 3 | History detective | What a source tells us, what it can't, and who made it and why (for example monks writing about Viking raids; the cakes legend written down long after Alfred) | source, choice |
| 4 | Think like a historian | Change and continuity, cause and consequence, significance, legend or fact | choice, sort |

### Age settings

Story cards of at most 70 words with sentences of at most 18 words; five rounds per run
from a bank of at least seven; three options per choice (shared spec §3).

### Chronology for 8–9 year olds

- One timeline runs across both units: Romans leave (about 410), Anglo-Saxon kingdoms,
  the Lindisfarne raid (793), the Great Heathen Army (865), Alfred (871–899), Athelstan
  (927), Cnut (1016), Edward the Confessor (1042–1066).
- Year 3's bands (Stone Age to Romans) appear faded on the left so the two years read as
  one story.
- Years and AD appear as labels; children order them and place them in bands, but never
  calculate with them.

### Tone for this year

The shared rules (shared spec §5) at an 8–9 level:

- Viking raids: "Vikings attacked the monastery, took its treasure and carried some
  people away to be sold as slaves."
- Battles are named with who won, not how people died.
- Trial by ordeal: "Sometimes a person had to pass a painful test, called an ordeal. People
  believed God would show if they were telling the truth." No description of the test.
- Vikings are shown as traders, farmers and settlers as well as raiders; the
  "who wrote this and why?" questions in slot 3 address the one-sided monk accounts.

These sentences were approved by the owner on 2026-10-12.

### Likely images

Lindisfarne Gospels and Anglo-Saxon Chronicle pages from British Library copies marked
public domain; Met and Cleveland CC0 Viking and Anglo-Saxon metalwork and coins; a
drawn Sutton Hoo helmet and Alfred Jewel (museum photographs of these are not CC0);
drawn maps of the kingdoms and the Danelaw. All must meet shared spec §6.
