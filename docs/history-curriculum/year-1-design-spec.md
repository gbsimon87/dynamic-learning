# Year 1 History: design spec

**Status:** Approved by the owner, 2026-10-12.
**Tracker:** [HISTORY_TRACKER.md](HISTORY_TRACKER.md). Read it first when resuming.
**Shared spec:** [shared-design-spec.md](shared-design-spec.md): engine, images, tone,
registration and testing for all years. This file holds only what is specific to Year 1.
**Source:** [key-stage-1-history.md](../curriculum/key-stage-1-history.md) (statutory, verbatim).

## 1. Goal

Year 1 History in Curriculum Mode: five categories, 14 topics, 56 challenges, for
children aged 5–6 who are only starting to read.

**Success means:** a Year 1 child can choose History, work through all 14 topics in
order with a grown-up nearby or alone using 🔊, and earn progress, rewards and
certificates as in other subjects; every question is answerable from its story card
and picture; every fact traces to a reference; every image has a recorded open licence.

## 2. The KS1 split (approved 2026-10-12)

Both Years 1 and 2 teach all four KS1 strands; see the split table in the
[Year 2 spec](year-2-design-spec.md) §2. Year 1 starts close to the child (their
grown-ups' and grandparents' childhoods) and reaches back to a few famous people and one
famous event.

| Strand (statutory) | Year 1 |
|---|---|
| changes within living memory | Toys, homes, school and seaside holidays: then and now |
| events beyond living memory that are significant nationally or globally | The first aeroplane flight |
| the lives of significant individuals … Some should be used to compare aspects of life in different periods | Explorers: Christopher Columbus and Neil Armstrong. Monarchs: Elizabeth I and Queen Victoria |
| significant historical events, people and places in their own locality | Clues around you: old buildings and street names |

## 3. Topics (approved 2026-10-12)

Titles become permanent progress keys once shipped (shared spec §8). Topics are listed
in release order.

### Then and Now

Statutory: *changes within living memory. Where appropriate, these should be used to
reveal aspects of change in national life*

| # | Icon | Topic | Content boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🧸 | Toys Then and Now | Toys grandparents played with (wooden, tin, dolls, marbles) and what is new today (apps, screens). Today's grandparents also had plastic and battery toys, so never present those as "today only" |
| 2 | 🏠 | Homes Then and Now | Coal fires, washing by hand, no TV or one channel; central heating, washing machines, screens |
| 3 | 🏫 | School Then and Now | Chalk and blackboards, rows of desks, school milk; whiteboards and tablets today |
| 4 | 🏖️ | Seaside Holidays Then and Now | Piers, Punch and Judy, donkeys, bathing huts long ago; holidays today |

### The First Flight

Statutory: *events beyond living memory that are significant nationally or globally

| # | Icon | Topic | Content boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | ✈️ | The Wright Brothers' First Flight | Orville and Wilbur Wright, 1903, a short flight of 12 seconds, the Wright Flyer |
| 6 | 🛩️ | Flying Then and Now | The first aeroplane compared with planes today: size, passengers, how far they go |

### Explorers

Statutory: *the lives of significant individuals … Some should be used to compare

| # | Icon | Topic | Content boundary |
|---|---|---|---|

| 9 | 🧭 | Explorers Then and Now | Comparing their journeys: ships and rockets, maps and computers, how long they took |

### Kings and Queens

Statutory: *the lives of significant individuals … [for example, Elizabeth I and Queen Victoria …]*

| # | Icon | Topic | Content boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 👑 | Queen Elizabeth I | Queen for a long time, the Armada, her clothes and portraits |

## 4. Learning design

Uses the shared model: story card, then rounds (shared spec §3).

### The four challenges

| Slot | Working name | Focus | Main rounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meet it | Who or what; key words with pictures | choice, sort |
| 2 | Old or new? | Old and new; then and now; first, next, last | sort, order |
| 3 | Picture detective | What a photo or painting shows us, and what it can't | source |
| 4 | Same or different? | Compare then and now, or two people | choice, sort |

### Age settings

Story cards of at most 30 words with sentences of at most 10 words; four rounds per run
from a bank of at least six; two or three options per choice; 🔊 on the story card and
every round, and every option has a picture where possible (shared spec §3). Wording
follows the Year 1 English rules: short, everyday and decodable where possible.

### Chronology for 5–6 year olds

- No years are shown or asked.
- Timeline bands, oldest on the left: "Long, long ago" (Columbus, Elizabeth I), "Long
  ago" (Queen Victoria, the first flight), "When grandparents were little" (Neil
  Armstrong, the "then" toys, homes and schools), "Today".
- Time words taught and used: old, new, then, now, before, after, first, next, last,
  long ago, a long time ago, today.

### Tone for this year

The shared rules (shared spec §5), at their gentlest. No deaths, wars or punishments are
described. One topic needs a plain, short truth:

- Columbus: "Columbus sailed to lands where people already lived. He and his men
  treated those people very unfairly."
- Elizabeth I and the Armada: "Her ships stopped the Spanish ships from landing." No
  fighting described.

These sentences were approved by the owner on 2026-10-12.

### Local history activity cards

Topics 13 and 14 end with an optional, collapsed grown-up card, as in Year 2 (for
example "On a walk with a grown-up, find the oldest building you can. What clues tell
you it is old?"). Never tracked; never gates progress.

### Likely images

Smithsonian and Met CC0 old toys; Library of Congress Photochrom prints of British
seaside towns about 1900; the 1903 Wright Flyer photograph; NASA Apollo 11 photographs
(public domain); portraits of Columbus (none painted from life, which is itself a
"picture detective" point), Elizabeth I and Queen Victoria. Everyday objects from
grandparents' childhoods (1960s–1970s) are mostly still in copyright as photographs, so
draw them. All must meet shared spec §6.
