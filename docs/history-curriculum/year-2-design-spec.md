# Year 2 History: design spec

**Status:** Approved by the owner, 2026-10-12.
**Tracker:** [HISTORY_TRACKER.md](HISTORY_TRACKER.md). Read it first when resuming.
**Shared spec:** [shared-design-spec.md](shared-design-spec.md): engine, images, tone,
registration and testing for all years. This file holds only what is specific to Year 2.
**Source:** [key-stage-1-history.md](../curriculum/key-stage-1-history.md) (statutory, verbatim).

## 1. Goal

Year 2 History in Curriculum Mode: six categories, 17 topics, 68 challenges, for
children aged 6–7.

**Success means:** a Year 2 child can choose History, work through all 17 topics in
order, and earn progress, rewards and certificates as in other subjects; every question
is answerable from its story card (read aloud) and source; every fact traces to a
reference; every image has a recorded open licence.

## 2. The KS1 split (approved 2026-10-12)

The curriculum sets out key stage 1 for Years 1 and 2 together, with four statutory
strands. Both years teach all four; Year 2 goes further back in time and deeper.

| Strand (statutory) | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| changes within living memory | Toys, homes, school, seaside holidays | Keeping in touch; getting about |
| events beyond living memory that are significant nationally or globally | The first aeroplane flight | The Great Fire of London; Bonfire Night and Remembrance Day (*events commemorated through festivals or anniversaries*) |
| the lives of significant individuals … Some should be used to compare aspects of life in different periods | Columbus and Armstrong; Elizabeth I and Victoria | Nightingale, Seacole and Cavell; Rosa Parks and Emily Davison; Caxton and Berners-Lee |
| significant historical events, people and places in their own locality | Clues around you | Memorials and blue plaques; old maps and photos |

The app cannot know where a child lives, so the local strand teaches **how to find
history near you**, with UK examples, plus an optional grown-up activity card (see §4).

Of the bracketed examples, Year 2 does not use Pieter Bruegel the Elder and LS Lowry:
Lowry's paintings are still in copyright, which breaks the image rule (shared spec §6).
Bracketed examples are non-statutory, so this leaves no statutory gap.

## 4. Learning design

Uses the shared model: story card, then rounds (shared spec §3).

### The four challenges

The slots follow the KS1 introduction: words for the passing of time, a chronological
framework, similarities and differences, asking and answering questions, how we find out
about the past and how it is represented.

| Slot | Working name | Focus | Main rounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meet it | Who, what, where; key words | choice, sort |
| 2 | Long ago or now? | Order of events; older and newer; then and now | order, sort, timeline |
| 3 | History detective | What a picture, object or diary tells us, and what it can't | source |
| 4 | Compare and remember | Same and different across times; why we still remember them | choice, sort |

### Age settings

Story cards of at most 45 words with sentences of at most 12 words; five rounds per run
from a bank of at least seven; three options per choice; 🔊 on every round, as Year 2
readers are still building fluency (shared spec §3).

### Chronology for 6–7 year olds

- Timeline bands, oldest on the left: "Long, long ago" (Caxton, the Great Fire, the
  Gunpowder Plot), "Long ago" (Nightingale, Seacole), "About 100 years ago"
  (Cavell, Davison, the First World War), "When grandparents were young" (Rosa Parks,
  1955), "Today".
- A few famous years appear as labels only (1605, 1666, 1918). They are never compared
  or calculated with.
- Time words taught and used: before, after, then, now, past, present, long ago, old, new,
  modern, a hundred years.

### Tone for this year

The shared rules (shared spec §5), tightened for 6–7 year olds. A death is mentioned only
when it is central to why someone is remembered, in one plain sentence with no detail:

- Edith Cavell: "In 1915 she was put to death for helping soldiers escape."
- Emily Davison: "At a horse race in 1913 she ran onto the track to protest. She was
  badly hurt and died."
- Remembrance: "Many soldiers died in the war. Poppies help us remember them."
- Gunpowder Plot: "A group of men planned to blow up Parliament and the King. Guy Fawkes
  was caught guarding the gunpowder." Nothing about what happened to the plotters.
- Rosa Parks: "In some places in America, unfair laws kept Black people and white people
  apart."
- Great Fire: very few deaths were recorded; the story is about homes lost and rebuilding.

These sentences were approved by the owner on 2026-10-12; they set the level for the whole year.

### Local history activity cards

Topics 16 and 17 end with an optional, collapsed grown-up card (the
`PracticalActivityCard` pattern from Science), for example "With a grown-up, find a war
memorial or blue plaque near you. Who does it remember?" It is never tracked and never
gates progress.

### Likely images

Rijksmuseum prints of the Great Fire, a Pepys portrait, the Monument, St Paul's;
Nightingale, Seacole and Cavell photographs of their time; Library of Congress
suffragette posters and Rosa Parks photographs with no known restrictions; a Caxton-era
printed page; a Gunpowder Plot print of 1605; a war memorial and poppies. Tim
Berners-Lee is drawn (shared spec §6). Every image must meet shared spec §6; if no
CC0 or public-domain copy exists, draw it.
