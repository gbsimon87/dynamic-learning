# Reaching the first families in Poole

Practical steps for ideas #70–76 in [PROJECT_IDEAS.md](PROJECT_IDEAS.md).
Start with parents and carers of children aged 6–8 in Poole and nearby areas.
The first trial aims to find five families willing to try one activity and
describe the experience. This is a working target, not a predicted response rate.

## Prepare one invitation

Use one existing activity rather than asking people to explore the whole app.
Start with [Number Bonds](https://learnwithbix.com/skills/number-bonds): select
target 10 and Match cards, then try one board together. Allow about five minutes;
stopping early is useful feedback too. It works without creating an account.
Use a different activity if a parent's stated interest makes it a better fit.

Before sharing, open that exact link signed out on a phone and finish a board.
The route and controls are confirmed in source; that is not live device testing.
Take one screenshot showing the game, with no child's profile or personal details.
Check that the inbox you offer for replies is monitored. The website publishes
`hello@learnwithbix.com`; test incoming mail before using it in an invitation.
An existing message thread is enough for the first families: a feedback form,
video, flyer or new landing page need not hold up these conversations.

Use a specific description: "a number-matching game to practise pairs that add
up to 10." Describe yourself as its creator and explain that these are the first
family trials. Avoid claims of proven learning results or school endorsement.

## Messages to adapt and send

Replace brackets and personalise the opening. The templates are drafts for the
owner to send; no outreach has been sent as part of preparing this guide.

### Someone you already know

> Hi [name], I've been building Learn with Bix and I'm beginning the first
> family trials. Would you and [your child] be willing to try this number-pairs
> game together for about five minutes this week?
>
> https://learnwithbix.com/skills/number-bonds
>
> Choose target 10 and Match cards. No account is needed. Afterwards, I'd love
> to know what they understood straight away and where either of you got stuck.
> Would it be OK to check in with you next week?

For an introduction: "Do you know a parent of a 6–8-year-old who might enjoy
trying it? Please forward this message if you think it fits. They can contact
me if interested."

### A group admin or home-education organiser

> Hi [name], I'm Simon, the creator of Learn with Bix. I'm looking for a few
> Poole-area families with children around 6–8 to try one short learning game
> and tell me what works or gets confusing.
>
> Would this kind of invitation be welcome in [group], and is there a particular
> thread or day I should use? Here's the activity for you to preview:
> https://learnwithbix.com/skills/number-bonds
>
> It needs no account. I'll make clear that I created it and keep the invitation
> to one post in the format you allow.

Once permitted, post:

> Hi, I'm Simon and I made Learn with Bix. I'm inviting parents and carers in
> Poole to try a short number-matching game with a child aged around 6–8.
>
> Try it here: https://learnwithbix.com/skills/number-bonds
> Choose target 10 and Match cards. No account needed; allow about five minutes.
>
> I'm interested in an honest first impression: was it clear what to do, and
> did your child want another go? You can reply here or message me privately.

Keep feedback about the experience; do not ask people to identify their child
in a public comment. Personalise a home-education invitation around optional
practice or the child's interests, rather than assumptions about school years.

## A manageable first two weeks

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| First session, about one hour | Check the activity on your phone; take one screenshot; send individual invitations to three suitable adults you know, or ask for introductions. |
| Next session | Personalise and send the Courthill PTA enquiry and one library enquiry. Find one relevant parent or home-education group and contact its admin. |
| When someone agrees | Send the direct game link and two setup instructions. Ask whether they prefer to try it privately or have you present for a short session. Agree whether you may check in later. |
| Over the following week | Hear from the first families. Fix any blocker that prevents starting or playing before inviting a larger group. Share the approved group post when the admin permits it. |
| After five to seven days | Send one follow-up to an unanswered organisational enquiry, unless its published policy asks otherwise. Leave it there if there is still no reply. Follow up with families only as agreed. |
| End of the second week | Compare the routes by actual family trials and what people said. Approach BCP Mumbler or a second organiser with the clearer invitation. Five trials is a useful aim, not a deadline to pressure people. |

Organiser follow-up: "Hello, just checking whether the family game-trial enquiry
below is suitable for [organisation]. If another person handles this, could you
point me towards them? Thanks, Simon."

Agreed family follow-up: "Did you get a chance to try it? If so, where did either
of you get stuck, and have you opened it again since? If you didn't try it, was
there anything that made it inconvenient or unappealing?"

## Learn from the replies

Keep a private working note of the organisation or adult contact, introduction
route, next agreed action and brief feedback. Keep personal contact details
outside the repository. The ideas file remains upcoming work, not a campaign log.
Ask adults where they heard about Bix; new analytics are not needed for this trial.

Count willing families, reported attempts, completed boards and voluntary return
visits separately. Missing replies are unknown, not failed sessions. These are
observations about usability and interest, not proof of learning outcomes.

- **No replies:** ask a contact whether the invitation was clear and relevant;
  try a warmer introduction or a different organiser before adding features.
- **Replies but few attempts:** ask what stopped them opening the activity;
  shorten the ask or offer a convenient time to try it together.
- **People start but get stuck:** fix the repeated instruction or interaction
  problem before extending outreach.
- **People finish but do not return:** ask what would give it a useful place in
  their week, and which activity they would actually choose next.
- **Someone finds it useful:** ask whether they would like a second relevant
  activity, or would feel comfortable forwarding the public link to one family.

Keep invitations and feedback adult-facing. Account and consent work remains in
#11 and analytics in #36; use their existing review scope when extending the
trial to account-based curriculum use or additional data collection.
